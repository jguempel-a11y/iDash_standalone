VA Label Types & Application SOP

Standard Operating Procedure and Infection Control Justification for the application of Omni-ID IQ350 RFID Labels on hospital equipment within VA clinical environments (Biomedical, ISO, Logistics, IT).

1. Infection Control Justification

The Omni-ID IQ350 is a synthetic, non-porous, on-metal RFID label designed for durable asset tracking in healthcare environments. It is safe for use and meets VA infection control expectations based on the following justifications:

Material & Durability

  • Non-Porous Surface: Sealed synthetic material does not absorb fluids or harbor biological material.
  • IP68 Rated: Fully sealed against liquids and particulates.
  • Passive RFID: Emits no power and does not affect device operation.

Cleaning Compatibility

Withstands routine wipe-downs with standard VA disinfectants:

  • Quaternary ammonium wipes (Sani-Cloth)
  • Isopropyl alcohol wipes (CaviWipes)
  • Bleach and Hydrogen Peroxide wipes
Restricted Uses: The IQ350 label must NOT be used on surgical instruments, in sterile fields, on patient-touch surfaces, or in high-heat/autoclave environments.

2. Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

Applying RFID labels in clinical environments requires strict adherence to hygiene and proper placement guidelines.

Pre-Application Requirements

  • Ensure the equipment is non-sterile and non-patient contact.
  • Clean the application surface thoroughly with 70% isopropyl alcohol.
  • Ensure the surface is dry, smooth, non-porous, and free of oils/residue.

Placement Guidelines

✔ Preferred Locations

  • Side panels or backs of devices
  • Metal housings and battery doors
  • Undersides of carts or equipment frames

✘ Surfaces to Avoid

  • Handles, keypads, and touchscreens
  • Patient touch areas
  • Any sterile field equipment

Application Steps

  1. Clean surface with alcohol and allow it to dry completely.
  2. Remove the label backing.
  3. Align the label on the cleaned surface and apply it firmly.
  4. Press down for 10–15 seconds, ensuring all edges are fully adhered.
  5. Allow the adhesive to cure for 12–24 hours before subjecting it to heavy cleaning.

Routine Inspection

During Preventative Maintenance (PM) or routine inventory sweeps, check labels for peeling edges. A peeling label can trap contaminants and must be replaced immediately. Document the asset ID, new label serial number, application date, and location.

3. VA Friendly FAQ

Q: Is the IQ350 safe for use in hospital environments?
Yes. It is a non-porous, synthetic, wipe-clean RFID label designed specifically for hospital and laboratory asset tracking.
Q: Can it be used on equipment in sterile fields or surgical instruments?
No. It is not designed for autoclave or high-heat sterilization.
Q: Will it interfere with equipment cleaning protocols?
No. It withstands routine wipe-downs and does not degrade under normal disinfectant use (Sani-Cloth, CaviWipes, Bleach, Peroxide).
Q: Are the adhesives medical-grade?
The adhesives are industrial-grade for secure equipment bonding, and are not intended for skin or direct patient contact.
Q: What if the label begins to peel?
Replace it immediately to avoid creating a trap for contaminants.
Q: Is this label used elsewhere in VA hospitals?
Yes, the IQ350 is widely deployed across multiple VA facilities for general asset tracking.
